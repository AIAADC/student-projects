# Citations, figures, tables, and rights

Read this reference whenever the manuscript uses external ideas, quotations, facts, data, tables, diagrams, screenshots, photographs, maps, or other media.

## Citation system

Use the current applicable edition of `GB/T 7714`; at the 2026-08-18 snapshot this is `GB/T 7714—2025`, effective 2026-07-01 and replacing the 2015 edition. Confirm whether the recipient requires sequential numeric or author-year citations. Do not mix systems.

The 2025 edition covers conventional and newer resource types, including books, journal articles, proceedings, theses, reports, standards, patents, websites/pages, archives, maps, datasets, preprints, databases, programs, and online resources. Use the entry pattern in the actual standard or recipient style, not memory from the 2015 edition.

### Citation ledger

For every cited item record:

```text
cite key | source type | creators | title | container | edition/version | place/publisher | date | volume/issue/pages | DOI/permanent ID | URL | access date | cited locator | verified source
```

Verify metadata against the work itself, its publisher, DOI registration record, repository, patent/standard database, or another authoritative catalog. Search results and AI summaries are discovery aids, not citation sources.

### In-text integrity

- Place the marker immediately after the supported claim or quotation, following the chosen punctuation/recipient rules.
- Give a page, section, figure, table, timestamp, or other locator for direct quotations and whenever the reader needs one to verify the use.
- Reuse the same identity for repeated citations; do not create duplicate bibliography entries for the same version.
- Distinguish a work from a version, translation, preprint, dataset release, corrected article, or archived web page.
- Every in-text citation must resolve to one bibliography entry; every bibliography entry must be cited unless the recipient explicitly allows a reading list.
- Keep citations attached to the claim during editing; update all fields before export.
- Do not cite a source for a stronger claim than the source supports.

### Reference-list integrity

- Preserve the source language and authoritative spelling/transliteration.
- Keep creators in the required order; distinguish personal and corporate creators.
- Capture the resource type and carrier where required.
- Include DOI or another persistent identifier when required or available under the chosen style.
- For changing online resources, record creation/update information and the access date as required by the current standard.
- Use one punctuation pattern, name treatment, date representation, and capitalization policy throughout.
- Do not invent missing place, publisher, issue, pages, DOI, or access date. Flag or resolve them.

## Quotations and paraphrases

Quotation marks, block quotation layout, and page locators do different jobs from a bibliography entry. Mark a direct quotation as a quotation and cite its exact location. A paraphrase still requires a citation when the idea, data, structure, or distinctive analysis is borrowed. Keep quotation extent proportionate to the explanatory purpose.

## Figure rules

For academic publishing, `CY/T 171—2019` is the detailed current industry reference. It states, among other things, that:

- an illustration generally consists of the figure, figure number, title, and notes;
- figures mentioned in the text are numbered and called out with that same number;
- numbering may be continuous or chapter-based, but must be consistent with tables/formulas;
- even a single figure is numbered;
- a title should identify the content accurately and concisely;
- a whole-figure note may explain the figure's source;
- figures should normally appear after their first callout, without breaking a natural paragraph or crossing chapters;
- figure number/title normally sit below the figure; notes have defined relative positions;
- cited figures require written permission from the rightsholder and source attribution;
- figures must remain legible, internally consistent, scientifically meaningful, and consistent with text terminology, numbers, symbols, and units.

For non-publishing reports, use these as declared editorial guidance unless a recipient rule says otherwise.

### Figure source categories

Use precise source notes:

- `作者绘制。`
- `作者根据文献[8]数据绘制。`
- `改绘自文献[12]，已获许可。`
- `转载自文献[15]，© 权利人，已获许可。`
- `数据来源：……；制图：作者。`
- `截图来源：某系统 2.3.1 版，2026-08-18；已对个人信息作脱敏处理。`

Do not use `来源：网络`, `百度图片`, `图片来自互联网`, or `侵删`. They are neither traceable citations nor permissions.

### Permission and lawful use

The Copyright Law of the People's Republic of China permits limited use in specified circumstances, including appropriate quotation of a published work for introduction, comment, or explanation, but the law requires attribution and prohibits interference with normal exploitation or unreasonable harm to the rightsholder's interests. Whether a whole photograph, chart, or artwork is an “appropriate quotation” is context-dependent. For formal publication or distribution, obtain written permission when required; `CY/T 171—2019` explicitly requires it for another person's illustration.

Treat these as separate questions:

1. Who owns the copyright or related rights?
2. What license, permission, statutory exception, or public-domain basis allows this use?
3. What attribution wording and link are required?
4. May the image be cropped, annotated, recolored, translated, or adapted?
5. Does the image contain people, private information, trademarks, restricted locations, cultural property, or regulated map content?

Save permission evidence and license terms in the project evidence folder. If permission is uncertain, remove the image, replace it with an original/licensed alternative, or stop for direction.

## Maps and sensitive imagery

Do not treat a web map screenshot as an ordinary decorative image. Verify map approval/attribution requirements, boundaries, place names, scale, source, and applicable map-review rules. Preserve required provider attribution. For people, medical records, IDs, chat messages, internal systems, and unpublished data, use authorized test data or proper anonymization; a caption does not cure a privacy breach.

## Tables

For academic publishing, `CY/T 170—2019` is the detailed current industry reference. Apply these stable principles:

- every table has a number, a concise title, and a textual callout;
- numbering is continuous or chapter-based and consistent with figures/formulas; even one table is numbered;
- use clear column headings; do not use a diagonal slash to combine incompatible headings;
- show quantities and units consistently, commonly in the form `quantity or symbol / unit symbol` where applicable;
- define abbreviations, symbols, statistical measures, and special cases in notes;
- mark data sources and permissions for reproduced/adapted tables;
- do not use a screenshot of an editable table unless the evidentiary image itself is the subject;
- use repeated header rows and accessible reading order for multi-page tables.

When deriving a table from multiple sources, cite the source for each relevant row/column or give a precise table note. Do not write only `资料整理` if external data were used.

## Figure/table production ledger

```text
ID | kind | original file | creator/rightsholder | license/permission evidence | source citation | transformation | callout | caption | source note | alt text | final page
```

Before delivery verify:

- all numbers are unique and sequential under the selected system;
- every item is called out before or near its placement;
- no callout points to a missing item;
- figure, caption, notes, and source stay together;
- images preserve aspect ratio and required resolution;
- text within figures is readable at final placed size;
- colors remain distinguishable in print/grayscale when needed;
- alternative text conveys purpose, not merely appearance;
- rights and privacy status are resolved.
